Technical Specification: End-to-End Predictive Architecture for Football Goal-Scoring ProbabilityThe Warsaw Econometric Challenge (WEC) 2026 demands a highly sophisticated integration of domain-specific football analytics and advanced machine learning engineering. The fundamental objective of the hackathon is to predict the binary target variable indicating whether a player will score a goal after a specific fifteen-minute match checkpoint. This task is inherently complex due to the severe rarity of the event, with the positive class representing merely 5.8 percent of the 3,486 total observations across 31 matches and 869 unique player appearances. Resolving this classification challenge requires an architecture that bridges raw event-level tracking data with highly regularized, hardware-accelerated machine learning models.The subsequent technical specification outlines an exhaustive blueprint for deploying an end-to-end predictive pipeline. It details the methodologies required to synthesize supplementary event datasets—encompassing shots, high-speed runs, passes, and actions under defensive pressure—into the temporally constrained main dataset. Furthermore, it provides strict operational parameters for executing deep feature engineering, optimizing hybrid gradient boosting and foundational tabular networks, and leveraging the massive parallel processing capabilities of an NVIDIA RTX 4090 while maintaining portability for localized ARM-based development.1. Feature Engineering Strategy: The Competitive EdgeThe provided base dataset encapsulates aggregated fifteen-minute rolling and cumulative match states, offering a baseline measure of physical exertion and shot volume. However, the true predictive power required to identify goal-scoring probability lies within the granular, event-level supplementary files. Engineering features that accurately capture spatial progression, cognitive decision-making under duress, and dynamic physical fatigue is essential to separating random variance from deterministic offensive capability. The feature extraction logic must transform discrete, isolated match events into continuous signals of player momentum and tactical influence.1.1 Expected Threat Formulation for Three-Zone Spatial ModelsTraditional football analytics rely heavily on Expected Goals metrics to value shot quality, but this approach fails to measure the vital buildup play that facilitates scoring opportunities. Expected Threat models provide a mathematical framework to evaluate possession-based actions by quantifying how much a specific pass or dribble increases a team's probability of scoring within the current possession sequence. Standard Expected Threat implementations rely on high-resolution spatial coordinates, typically dividing the football pitch into a sophisticated grid array, such as sixteen by twelve zones, to calculate granular spatial transition probabilities.Because the provided supplementary dataset detailing pass events abstracts spatial coordinates into three distinct longitudinal stages—defensive third, central area, and attacking third—the traditional high-resolution spatial model must be adapted into a discrete, three-state Markov decision process. The expected threat of possessing the ball in any specific zone is defined conceptually as the sum of the immediate shot threat and the potential downstream move threat. The mathematical formulation requires calculating the probability of taking a shot from a specific zone, multiplied by the probability of that shot resulting in a goal, and adding this to the probability of moving the ball, which is multiplied by the transition probabilities to all other zones and their respective threat values.To compute these spatial values for the automated pipeline, the extraction logic must be rigorously applied across the limited shot dataset and the pass event dataset. First, the algorithm must initialize the threat values for all three zones to zero. Subsequently, it must filter the passing dataset for accurate passes and compute a transition matrix by calculating the historical proportion of successful passes originating in a specific zone that terminate in each of the available zones. While destination zones are not explicitly provided in the provided passing log schema, they can be probabilistically inferred through sequential event analysis or by assigning a standardized spatial progression value based on the average receiving zone of the intended recipient.The implementation logic must iterate the Expected Threat equation across the transition matrix until the values converge, which typically requires four to five computational iterations. Once convergence is achieved, the feature factory module must calculate the net threat added for each player during the preceding fifteen-minute window and cumulatively across the match. The threat added by a single progressive action is the difference between the value of the destination zone and the value of the origin zone. A player's ability to maintain possession and progress the play under these hostile conditions is a highly predictive indicator of offensive efficacy and elite technical composure.The feature engineering pipeline must extract specific metrics of press resistance to capture the cognitive and technical performance of players when the spatial constraints of the pitch are artificially minimized by the defending team. The foremost metric to engineer is the Press Retention Rate, which evaluates the ratio of pressure events resulting in positive tactical outcomes—such as forward passes, lateral passes, or ball carries—against the total volume of pressure events applied to the player. This metric isolates composure from raw volume.To provide deeper tactical granularity, the pipeline must calculate Progressive Press Evasion. This involves a specific count of forward pass and ball carry outcomes executed strictly under pressure, demonstrating a player's ability to not merely survive a defensive press, but to exploit the structural imbalances created by the pressing opposition to break defensive lines. Furthermore, Pass Angle Distortion can be engineered by comparing the standard deviation of a player's pass angle when pressed against their baseline pass angle in unpressed situations. This metric quantifies whether a player's vision and technical execution are narrowed by defensive proximity, a strong indicator of cognitive overload.To synthesize these behavioural indicators, the architecture should implement a variation of Expected Pass Turnovers. Utilizing the overall dataset's baseline turnover rate under pressure segmented by pitch zone, an expected turnover probability is assigned to each individual action. By subtracting a player's actual observed turnover count from their expected turnovers, the feature factory yields a continuous metric of exceptional press resistance. These highly contextualized cognitive metrics must be aggregated into the rolling fifteen-minute and cumulative prefixes to seamlessly merge with the main dataset based on the unique player appearance identifier and the temporal checkpoint boundaries.1.3 Relative Intensity and Dynamic Fatigue ModelingThe physical exertion data contained within the high-speed run and sprint event logs provides the raw material necessary to construct sophisticated relative intensity models. Relying solely on raw cumulative distance or absolute count metrics fails to capture the true physiological state of a player, as individual baseline aerobic capacities and positional requirements vary drastically across the pitch.To accurately detect whether a player is experiencing acute physical fatigue or exhibiting high-impact momentum, the feature engineering logic must calculate an adapted Acute to Chronic Workload Ratio scaled to the precise ninety-minute temporal window of a football match. The sports science literature indicates that high-intensity running and sprinting metrics typically decline significantly in the final fifteen minutes of competitive matches due to accumulating neuromuscular fatigue, although some of this decline is structurally attributed to an increase in game interruptions rather than purely physiological degradation.To isolate genuine physiological pacing and fatigue, the intra-match relative intensity calculation must compare the acute workload—represented by the high-speed running metrics in the immediate fifteen-minute window prior to the checkpoint—against the chronic workload, which is defined as the player's average fifteen-minute physical output calculated from the cumulative metrics across the match up to that specific checkpoint. A relative intensity ratio that falls significantly below the equilibrium value of one indicates the onset of fatigue, which logically correlates negatively with the explosive offensive actions required to generate goal-scoring opportunities.Conversely, an intensity ratio that spikes significantly above baseline during the later stages of the second half indicates high-impact physical momentum. This phenomenon is often associated with late-game tactical shifts, the introduction of impactful substitutes, or a strategic surge in pressing intensity. Modeling Architecture for Small, Imbalanced DataThe structural dimensions of the main analytical dataset—containing approximately three and a half thousand rows with a severe 5.8 percent positive class representation—dictate highly specific and rigorous modeling constraints. Deep neural networks of traditional architectures will almost certainly suffer from catastrophic overfitting, while standard generalized linear models lack the representational capacity to capture the complex, non-linear, and multi-dimensional interactions between spatial progression, cognitive composure, and physical fatigue. Resolving this requires a hybrid ensemble architecture that utilizes zero-shot tabular foundation models alongside highly regularized gradient boosting frameworks.2.1 Foundational Integration of Tabular Prior-Data Fitted NetworksThe introduction of Tabular Prior-Data Fitted Networks, or TabPFN, represents a fundamental paradigm shift for the classification of small to medium-sized tabular datasets. Pre-trained offline on millions of synthetic datasets derived from structural causal models, TabPFN executes in-context Bayesian inference, predicting targets in a single forward pass of the transformer network without requiring any gradient-based optimization, backpropagation, or exhaustive hyperparameter tuning on the target dataset.The second iteration of TabPFN is explicitly bounded by specific architectural limits: it is optimally designed to scale up to ten thousand training samples and five hundred features. With exactly 3,486 rows, the Warsaw Econometric Challenge dataset falls perfectly within the operational regime where TabPFN consistently and significantly outperforms heavily tuned tree-based models on classification tasks. Its ability to seamlessly handle variable dataset sizes and automatically capture complex feature interactions via intra-feature attention mechanisms makes it an invaluable asset for predicting rare football events.However, the architecture possesses known mathematical limitations regarding severe class imbalance. TabPFN does not natively support sample weighting mechanisms, meaning the algorithm cannot be instructed to artificially inflate the importance of the 5.8 percent positive class during the forward pass calculation. To circumvent this structural limitation, the pipeline must discard the default binary threshold of one-half and instead strictly utilize the raw Bayesian posterior probabilities generated by the network. The decision boundary can then be optimized post-hoc using comprehensive precision-recall curve analysis to maximize the harmonic mean of precision and recall specifically for the minority goal-scoring class. Furthermore, the model can serve as an exceptionally powerful dense feature extractor; by utilizing the embedding transformer utility, the pipeline can condense the vast feature space into learned latent embeddings, which can then be passed downstream into traditional gradient boosting algorithms to enhance their performance.2.2 Gradient Boosting with Custom Focal Loss OptimizationTo construct a truly robust and resilient predictive ensemble, the zero-shot capabilities of TabPFN must be paired with the iterative refinement of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees, specifically leveraging advanced libraries such as CatBoost or XGBoost. Gradient boosting frameworks offer the fine-grained control over class imbalance that foundation models lack, allowing for deep penalization of minority class misclassifications. While utilizing a standard scale positive weight parameter is a common industry approach to artificially inflate the gradient updates of the minority class, it frequently results in poorly calibrated posterior probabilities and aggressive false positive rates, which would render the model strategically useless to a football coach attempting to assess genuine goal-scoring likelihood.A mathematically superior and much more elegant approach is the implementation of Focal Loss. This objective function introduces a dynamic scaling factor to the standard binary cross-entropy loss, aggressively down-weighting the loss assigned to easily classified majority examples and forcing the tree-building algorithm to focus its computational resources on hard-to-predict minority instances. The mathematical formulation introduces a modulating factor based on the model's estimated probability for the true class, controlled by a focusing parameter that dictates the steepness of the penalty for easy examples. Furthermore, a weighting factor is introduced to manage the baseline class imbalance, typically set proportional to the inverse class frequency to mathematically anchor the loss landscape.Implementing custom objective functions within hardware-accelerated environments introduces specific architectural conflicts that the automated coding pipeline must navigate. For instance, the default tree growth policy in CatBoost is the symmetric tree algorithm, which builds trees level by level to ensure highly optimized and rapid inference speeds on central processing units. However, attempting to utilize a custom focal loss objective on a graphical processing unit backend with symmetric tree growth throws a fatal optimization scheme error, as the specific combination of custom loss and symmetric architecture is unsupported in the CUDA implementation.To successfully compile and execute focal loss on the target NVIDIA RTX 4090 hardware, the Python pipeline must explicitly override the default tree growth policy parameters. The implementation must specify a loss guide growth policy, which shifts the algorithm to a leaf-wise growth strategy. Because the chronological dataset logs multiple players simultaneously across the exact same fifteen-minute match intervals, cross-validation data splits must be engineered with absolute precision to prevent devastating train-test contamination.If a standard randomized cross-validation strategy or a simple stratified splitting mechanism is utilized, data from the same specific match—for example, a high-scoring fixture experiencing heavy rainfall during the second half—could easily appear in both the training and the validation data partitions. A highly parameterized model could inadvertently learn to recognize the specific tactical context, the physical fatigue patterns, or the unique score state of that specific match, achieving artificially high validation scores that will immediately and catastrophically collapse upon deployment to unseen future matches.To strictly and reliably evaluate algorithmic generalization, the pipeline architecture must implement a grouped cross-validation strategy. The critical architectural decision for the machine learning engineer is selecting the appropriate grouping key: the unique player appearance identifier or the match fixture identifier. Grouping the data solely by the player appearance ensures the model generalizes to unseen players, but it fails to address the environmental leakage.Given that multiple players interact dynamically within the exact same tactical environment, the overarching match state creates a shared causal link to the goal-scoring probabilities of all individuals on the pitch. Therefore, implementing a grouped k-fold strategy keyed explicitly by the fixture identifier is the only mathematically sound approach. This strategy guarantees that the tactical flow, the weather conditions, the refereeing strictness, and the complex score dynamics of a specific ninety-minute event are entirely isolated within a single computational fold. Furthermore, all subsequent data scaling procedures, missing value imputations, and foundational model embedding generations must be instantiated strictly within the internal cross-validation loop to guarantee that no statistical information from the validation distribution leaks into the training parameter space.3. GPU-Accelerated Workflow StrategyThe computational environment defined for this predictive pipeline is inherently dual-faceted. The massive hyperparameter search spaces and the heavy event-level data aggregations will execute primarily on a high-end NVIDIA RTX 4090 boasting twenty-four gigabytes of video random access memory, while logical development, pipeline debugging, and unit testing will occur sequentially on an ARM-based MacBook architecture. The machine learning infrastructure must dynamically detect the prevailing hardware environment at runtime and intelligently route execution protocols accordingly.3.1 Data Preprocessing Acceleration via RAPIDS cuDFThe supplementary datasets provided for the hackathon encompass over seventy-seven thousand distinct event rows detailing passes, runs, shots, and defensive pressures. Iterating over these tens of thousands of rows using standard iterative loops or applying custom functions to grouped data structures in a traditional central processing unit environment to aggregate fifteen-minute rolling windows for hundreds of players is computationally prohibitive and creates a severe, unacceptable bottleneck during the iterative feature engineering phase.To shatter this bottleneck, the pipeline must leverage the RAPIDS cuDF library. This open-source framework provides a Python graphical processing unit DataFrame library built on the Apache Arrow columnar memory format, seamlessly mirroring the familiar Pandas application programming interface while executing operations via highly optimized underlying CUDA C++ kernels.Optimizing the integration of the RAPIDS framework requires specific architectural techniques. First, all rolling aggregations for the Expected Threat calculations, the sprint volumes, and the press retention metrics must be expressed mathematically as strictly vectorized grouping and aggregation operations. The cuDF library inherently and automatically parallelizes these vectorized operations across the thousands of available CUDA cores on the RTX 4090 architecture, reducing computation times from several minutes to mere milliseconds.Secondly, the engine must manage sort elimination. Unlike standard Pandas environments, which implicitly and automatically sort grouping keys during aggregation operations to maintain index order, cuDF defaults to disabling sorting to maximize the sheer speed of hash-based aggregations. This default parameter must be strictly maintained unless temporal sorting is explicitly and mathematically required to maintain the chronological integrity of the rolling windows. Finally, the pipeline must actively manage memory allocation. While the twenty-four gigabytes of video memory on the RTX 4090 is massive, inefficient multi-table merging and broad casting can lead to memory fragmentation. Operations must utilize unified memory management capabilities if the data structures expand, although the seventy-seven thousand rows of event data will comfortably fit into the primary video memory without requiring offloading to host memory. For the local ARM-based environment where CUDA acceleration is unsupported, the feature extraction module must utilize a dynamic wrapper that automatically falls back to standard multi-threaded libraries.3.2 Massive Hyperparameter Optimization via OptunaTo discover the optimal structural hyperparameters for the gradient boosting algorithms and to fine-tune the complex extraction thresholds for the feature factory, the architecture will utilize the Optuna hyperparameter optimization framework. Optuna leverages the highly efficient Tree-structured Parzen Estimator algorithm, which builds sequential probabilistic models of the objective function to intelligently focus sampling efforts on the most promising hyperparameter spaces rather than wasting computational cycles on random or grid-based searches.Integrating this optimization framework with graphical processing unit backends requires careful handling of software limitations. While Optuna natively supports pruning unpromising trials early in the training phase to save computational time, this specific callback functionality is fundamentally incompatible with the CatBoost graphical processing unit backend. The underlying CatBoost CUDA engine does not currently support the execution of user-defined Python callbacks during active hardware acceleration.Therefore, the automated pipeline must completely bypass standard callback pruning methodologies when operating on the RTX 4090 hardware. Instead, the architecture will rely on the sheer brute-force computational speed of the graphical processing unit to rapidly complete full training runs for every proposed parameter set. The objective function defined for the study must constrain the search space tightly around the most critical parameters: the focal loss gamma modifier to dictate the penalty for easy examples, the maximum tree depth to control interaction complexity without overwhelming the limited sample size, and the leaf regularization terms to forcefully combat overfitting on the three and a half thousand rows of training data.4. Pipeline Architecture SpecificationTo facilitate seamless execution and reliable automated code generation via an intelligent coding assistant, the entire solution must be structured into a highly modular, object-oriented Python package. The separation of concerns ensures that data loading, mathematical transformation, model training, and probabilistic ensembling remain strictly isolated.4.1 Data Ingestion Module (data_ingestion.py)This foundational module is exclusively responsible for bridging the raw comma-separated value files from the storage disk into active memory. It must include a robust environment detection protocol to intelligently load data into either accelerated graphical processing unit DataFrames or standard structures based on the detection of CUDA-visible devices. The module executes vital schema validation, ensuring that string-based boolean flags regarding pass accuracy and pressing outcomes are properly cast to memory-efficient data types, heavily reducing the memory footprint required for downstream operations. Furthermore, this module handles the initial temporal merging, utilizing the player appearance identifiers to map discrete events to the specific individuals participating in the match.4.2 Feature Factory Module (feature_factory.py)The feature factory is designed as a purely stateless mathematical transformation module. It exposes isolated functions responsible for calculating the Expected Threat additions, determining the intra-match relative intensity ratios, and aggregating the complex press resistance behavioural metrics. By maintaining a strictly stateless design, the module guarantees that no statistical information regarding the underlying data distribution is retained in memory between the various folds of the cross-validation process. It processes the raw event data, rigorously applies the specific rolling temporal windows mapped exactly to the local minutes of the match checkpoints, and outputs the final augmented feature matrix ready for algorithmic consumption.4.3 Trainer Module (trainer.py)The trainer module encapsulates the complex cross-validation logic and the primary model training loops. It implements the grouped k-fold strategy keyed strictly on the match fixture identifier to guarantee environmental isolation. The module contains the hyperparameter optimization study configuration and dynamically instantiates the gradient boosting models, automatically passing the required hardware task type flags and overriding the tree growth policies to enable leaf-wise growth when operating on the primary accelerated hardware. It also seamlessly manages the fallback execution protocols required for the local ARM-based testing architecture, ensuring code portability without requiring manual configuration alterations.4.4 Ensemble Module (ensemble.py)The final component of the pipeline defines the sophisticated mathematical blending strategy between the foundational transformer network and the gradient boosting outputs. Because the tabular prior-data fitted network outputs dense, well-calibrated probabilities based on broad in-context Bayesian inference , and the focal-loss-optimized gradient boosting models output predictions that are heavily skewed toward catching the extreme minority class , the predictions generated by the two architectures represent mathematically divergent perspectives on the underlying data. The ensemble module will utilize a logistic regression meta-learner, commonly referred to as a stacking classifier, trained exclusively on the out-of-fold predictions generated by the base models during the cross-validation phase. This meta-learner learns exactly how much to trust the Bayesian probabilities versus the focal-loss predictions, outputting the final, highly calibrated probability of a goal being scored.5. Strategic Leakage and Validity AuditThe dynamic, strictly chronological nature of the Warsaw Econometric Challenge dataset creates incredibly hazardous traps for inadvertent data leakage. Designing a pipeline to forecast future events based on rolling temporal snapshots requires rigid adherence to temporal constraints. The following validation rules must be unconditionally enforced throughout the codebase to ensure predictive integrity.5.1 Strict Temporal Event MaskingWhen executing relational joins to merge the supplementary event data containing passes and runs to the base checkpoint observations, the mathematical logic must strictly enforce that the minute the event occurred is less than or equal to the minute of the checkpoint. Any rolling feature calculation must aggressively discard all events that occur even a single second after the exact minute of the recorded checkpoint. Including a highly progressive pass that occurred at minute thirty-one for an observation checkpoint officially defined at minute thirty introduces lethal future-leakage, artificially and fatally inflating the model's apparent predictive capability during local testing.5.2 Algorithmic Outcome SanitizationThe dataset providing granular details regarding every shot taken during the tournament contains an outcome column that explicitly details whether the ball entered the net. This column must be completely and irreversibly sanitized and dropped from the training feature space before any extraction logic is applied. Because the ultimate target variable to be predicted is directly derived from whether a shot resulted in a goal, allowing the models, particularly the Expected Threat transition matrix generator, to access this column bypasses the entire predictive objective. The model would cease to search for the causal precursors to a goal and instead simply map the known outcome to the target, destroying its utility for forecasting future, unseen match scenarios.5.3 Rigorous Substitution BoundariesThe base dataset tracks exactly when players enter and exit the pitch using continuous temporal fields. These fields must act as strict, non-negotiable validity bounds during the data preparation phase. Checkpoints where the minute the player was substituted out occurs before the checkpoint minute, or where the minute the player was substituted in occurs after the checkpoint minute, must be subjected to aggressive auditing. The pipeline must ensure that players who are not physically and actively present on the pitch at the exact moment of the temporal observation are totally excluded from the rolling aggregations and the target predictions, as predicting the scoring probability for a player sitting on the bench introduces fatal noise into the evaluation metrics.5.4 Cross-Validation Pipeline IsolationFinally, the architecture must ensure that the test set distribution never influences the training parameters. All operations that utilize population-level statistics—such as standardizing numerical features by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation, imputing missing values using median representations, or generating complex latent embeddings using the foundational transformer—must occur strictly and exclusively within the active training fold of the cross-validation loop. If data is transformed globally before the data split occurs, the model inadvertently gains access to the statistical boundaries of the validation data, compromising the entire evaluation framework and resulting in profound performance degradation when deployed into a live, real-world analytical environment.
